---
Connor's teaching is grounded in inclusive pedagogy — a continuous, iterative practice of examining how the norms of university education can, often invisibly, exclude students who fall outside its traditional constituencies. He organizes his courses around three commitments: student comfort, clarity of expectations, and outcome-driven flexibility, calibrating each in conversation with the particular students in the room rather than a fixed standard.

On the first day of every course, he tells students that religious studies rewards good-faith attempts at understanding more than correct answers — a starting point for building a classroom where unfamiliar or destabilizing material can be safely explored, and where success is defined with each student rather than imposed on them.
